Nothing has been posted or sent. The launch is openly accessible on GitHub Pages:

## First month

- Before launch: ask 10–15 willing readers or teachers to flag confusing passages.
- Launch: share the cover, one interior illustration, the link, and why you wrote it.
- A few days later: teach one idea using a real illustration from the book.
- End of week one: share a 20–40 second recording of an interactive demo.
- Week two: post a short sequence: story scene, picture, worked example, book link.
- Weeks three and four: show a revision prompted by feedback, and ask which math
  or science topic should become the next story. Quote readers only with permission.

Add the website to your profile's Featured section. Reply to thoughtful comments.
Approach a few suitable educators individually. Your approximately 5,100 followers
are a starting audience; they do not guarantee 5,100 readers. Start with a concrete
learning goal, such as ten substantive pieces of reader feedback. Watch LinkedIn's
post impressions and clicks; this website does not track individual readers or
collect email addresses.

## Launch post draft

I've written my first book, and I'm making the complete first volume free.

It's an illustrated introduction to multilinear algebra, told through a journey
with Alice in Wonderland: arrows, changing coordinates, tensors, and differential
forms.

I wanted to explore a question: can a mathematical idea become easier to grasp
when we first meet it through a story and a picture?

The book includes twelve chapters, worked examples, exercises with hints and
answers, and twelve interactive demos. It is written for curious readers with
high-school mathematics.

This is the beginning of a planned series explaining mathematics and science
through stories. The first book is free; I intend future volumes to be paid.

Read online or download the complete PDF. No signup required:
https://fyodoramanov1.github.io/multilinear-algebra-book/

If you read a chapter, I'd love to hear which explanation helped you most—and
where the story needs to work harder.
