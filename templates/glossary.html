Glossary

Short, friendly reminders for the words used in the book.

Scalar

A number from a specified field: real numbers in the main journey, complex numbers in marked notes.

Vector

An element of a vector space. A real Euclidean arrow is a first example; length requires a measuring rule.

Basis

An independent spanning set giving every vector a unique list of scalar coefficients.

Covector and dual space

A covector is a linear scalar-valued question. All such questions form the dual vector space.

Linear map

A transformation that preserves addition and scaling.

Inner product

A positive-definite pairing defining length and orthogonality. Complex inner products involve conjugation.

Determinant

A scalar detecting whether a square matrix is invertible. Over the real numbers, it gives a signed volume factor.

Eigenvalue

A stretching factor associated with a special direction.

Bilinear form

A scalar-valued rule linear in each of two vector inputs separately.

Tensor

An element of a tensor-product space, describable in finite dimensions as a multilinear rule on appropriate vector and covector inputs.

Tensor type, order, and rank

Type distinguishes the kinds of slots; order counts them. Rank is the fewest pure products needed in a sum.

Wedge product

An alternating exterior product. Simple real vector wedges represent oriented patches.

Differential form

A smooth field of alternating covector tensors measuring oriented regions of the matching dimension.

Exterior derivative

The operator that takes a smooth form to the next degree and describes local change.

Traction

Force per unit area across a surface, given by stress acting on its unit normal.
